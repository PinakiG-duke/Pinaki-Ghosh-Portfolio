Background

A decade building analytical systems in retail, hospitality, and airline analytics that change how businesses decide. The work covered customer decision systems across loyalty bases of 5 to 10 million customers, pricing and promotion analytics, and experimentally verified incremental revenue impact across clients in the UK, Middle East, and South Asia. Outcomes were always measured against a held-out control.

Currently at Duke completing a Master in Data Science. Research sits at the intersection of sequential decision-making under uncertainty, causal inference, and human-in-the-loop system design.

Research

The central question is how much of the theoretical value of an adaptive decision system is realized when a human acts on its recommendations, and what design choices determine that fraction. Most retail allocation systems treat demand as a forecasting problem. The more fundamental problem is the decision gap — the difference between what an adaptive policy could deliver if allowed to update as uncertainty resolves, and what a static system actually delivers when it cannot.

The Economic Value of Adaptivity (EVA) formalizes this gap and decomposes it into two measurable components. SKUFlux is the four-layer system operationalizing this research. The human interface layer is the empirical core, grounded in ongoing experimental work under Professor Jana Schaich Borg at Duke.
