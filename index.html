Background

A decade building analytical systems in retail, hospitality, and airline analytics that change how businesses decide. The work covered customer decision systems across loyalty bases of 5 to 10 million customers, pricing and promotion analytics, and experimentally verified incremental revenue impact across clients in the UK, Middle East, and South Asia. Outcomes were always measured against a held-out control.

Currently at Duke completing a Master in Data Science. Research sits at the intersection of sequential decision-making under uncertainty, causal inference, and human-in-the-loop system design.

Research

The research program starts from a commercial observation: organizations with access to better algorithms do not consistently make better decisions. The gap is not algorithmic. It sits at the interface between the system and the decision maker.

The work spans four connected layers. Demand uncertainty quantification establishes how retail demand behaves across SKUs and environments — whether it has memory, when it shifts structurally, and what that means for adaptive policy design. Causal inference identifies the structural drivers of behavioral change, separating what can be acted on from what merely correlates. Optimization evaluates adaptive allocation policies against the theoretical maximum value achievable under real inventory and operational constraints. The human interface layer asks how much of that theoretical value is realized when a human decision maker acts on the system's recommendations, and what representation choices determine that fraction.

The Economic Value of Adaptivity (EVA) formalizes this end-to-end gap. SKUFlux is the four-layer system operationalizing the research in retail allocation. The empirical foundation for the human interface layer is ongoing experimental work under Professor Jana Schaich Borg at Duke.
