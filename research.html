01 Simulation

Characterizing demand uncertainty as the statistical foundation for adaptive policy evaluation. Establishing how demand persistence varies across SKUs and demand environments.

When Does Demand Have Memory?
STA 611, Duke University  ·  2026
Completed
+
Research Question

Is AR(1) demand persistence statistically significant and SKU-level across the retail catalog? Does it remain temporally stable, or does it exhibit regime shifts consistent with the demand environments posited in the EVA typology?

Findings

91.1% of sampled Walmart SKUs (M5 dataset, 5.3 years) show statistically significant AR(1) persistence (mean ρ = 0.550). One-way ANOVA confirms ρ is a SKU-level property, not predictable from product category taxonomy (F = 0.979, p = 0.384). Chow tests reveal 78.3% of high-persistence SKUs exhibit structural regime shifts — ρ collapse and amplification patterns map directly to DE3 and DE4 demand environments in the EVA typology. Updated demand specification incorporates time-varying, store-adjusted persistence with heavier-tailed errors.

02 Causal Inference

Identifying structural drivers of behavioral change using quasi-experimental methods. Establishing causal claims from observational data where randomized experiments are not available.

Early Evidence on Generative AI Exposure and Labor Market Outcomes
IDS 702, Duke University  ·  2026  ·  Team: Aesha Gandhi, Pinaki Ghosh, Shambhavi Khanna, Isaac Vergara
Completed
+
Research Question

Are early signs of differential labor market strain already measurable in AI-exposed industries, and are early-career workers bearing disproportionate pressure?

Findings

Difference-in-differences analysis using CPS ASEC data (2016–2025) linked to the AIIE industry-level AI exposure index (Felten et al. 2021). Post-2023 unemployment increased 0.29 percentage points in high-AIIE industries relative to low-AIIE industries, concentrated in workers aged 25–34. No comparable wage signal detected in the current post-period window. Event-study coefficients confirm directional robustness of the unemployment result. Findings inform early policy intervention targeting AI-exposed labour markets before displacement effects become conclusive.

03 Optimization

Designing and solving constrained allocation problems under real operational stakes. Building the optimization formulation depth underlying the SKUPlan layer of the EVA architecture.

Multi-Objective School Siting and Boundary Optimization
Durham Public Schools  ·  Duke Data+  ·  2026  ·  Graduate Research Lead
In Progress
+
Research Question

How should Durham County optimize new school siting and attendance boundary assignment across 851 residential planning units and 50 existing schools under projected enrollment uncertainty driven by residential development, demographic shifts, and charter expansion?

Findings

Extended a single-objective capacitated facility location baseline across four methodological tracks: road network distance improvement using OpenStreetMap, a systematic multi-metric solution evaluation framework, enrollment forecast combination using Student Generation Rate and Cohort Survival Rate methods, and a multi-objective Pareto frontier formulation via the epsilon-constraint method incorporating assignment stability and socioeconomic diversity alongside distance minimization. Primary author. Presenting at Duke Data+ Symposium, July 2026.

04 Human-Algorithm Interaction

Empirically establishing how representation choices in analytical systems affect the quality of human decisions. The direct empirical foundation for the EVA human interface layer.

Cognitive-Centered Data Visualization
Bass Connections, Duke University  ·  2025–26  ·  Supervisor: Prof. Jana Schaich Borg
Paper in Progress
+
Research Question

Do established data visualization best practices actually improve comprehension and decision-making? Specifically, do chart titles and data highlighting affect viewer interpretation, confidence, and the conclusions viewers draw?

Findings

Two completed Prolific-based factorial experiments (N = 476). Descriptive chart titles create systematic overconfidence in incorrect interpretations. Take-home titles dramatically increase correct conclusion rates. Findings challenge a foundational assumption in visualization guidance and establish the empirical basis for representation design choices in adaptive decision interfaces. A follow-up eye-tracking study is designed to validate Phase 1 findings through attentional mechanism analysis. Paper in progress. Research presented at the Duke Bass Connections Symposium, April 2026.
